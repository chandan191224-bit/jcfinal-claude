# Section playbook

Every script has four sections, always in this order when spoken:
**HOOK → BEGINNING → MID → CONCLUSION**.
They are **written** in a different order: Beginning → Mid → Conclusion → **Hook last**. The
creator writes the hook only after the whole story is done, because by then the strongest
shock is obvious.

## Length budget

Hindi narration runs at about **220 words per minute** (the reference script is about 1,500
words for roughly 6.5–7 minutes). Default target: **6–7 minutes ≈ 1,350–1,550 words.**

| Section | Share | Words (6–7 min) | Spoken time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | ~5–6% | 70–100 | 20–30 sec |
| Beginning | ~25–30% | 350–450 | ~1.5–2 min |
| Mid | ~50–55% | 700–850 | ~3–4 min |
| Conclusion | ~10–12% | 150–200 | ~45–60 sec |

For other lengths, scale every section by the same ratio (words ≈ minutes × 220). Always
report the word count and estimated time at the end.

---

## 1. HOOK (20–30 seconds)

**Job:** give a sudden jolt that makes leaving the video feel impossible, without asking
the viewer to stay.

Build it like this:
1. **Paradox or shocking fact first, with no warm-up.** It should sound unbelievable but be
   true. ("दो देशों के बीच का एक बॉर्डर जिसे आज तक किसी ने नहीं देखा।")
2. **One or two "और कैसे..." lines** that hint at the journey without giving away the answer:
   origin → twist → cost. ("और कैसे तिब्बत से शुरू हुई एक विवाद ने, हजारों साल पुरानी दोस्ती
   को दुश्मनी की आग में झोंक दिया।")
3. **Stakes in human terms:** lives, money, land, or the future. ("...हजारों सैनिकों की जान ले
   ली।")
4. **Signature launch line:** "चलिए बिना किसी time waste के शुरू करते हैं, <topic as a
   dramatic one-liner> की ये बेहद <adjective> story, हमारे साथ the favorite Show में।"
   This line is the show's branded intro, not a CTA. Keep its shape and vary the adjective
   (confusing / खौफनाक / अनसुनी / दिलचस्प ...).

Rules:
- The hook doesn't have to follow the order or pace of the script. It can pull the most
  shocking point from anywhere: a modern number, a strange fact, an irony.
- No like, subscribe, comment or "पूरा वीडियो देखिए" anywhere.
- No dates in the first line. Lead with the image or the paradox.
- Write 2–3 alternative hooks, put the strongest in the script, and list the others below
  the script as options.

## 2. BEGINNING (the deep backstory, topic not yet named)

**Job:** start far back, well before the topic, and walk forward so naturally that the viewer
reaches the topic without noticing the turn.

Build it like this:
1. **Entry line:** pull the viewer into the past. Examples: "इस पूरी कहानी को समझने के लिए हमे
   इतिहास के पन्नों में झांकना होगा दोस्तों।" / "ये कहानी शुरू होती है आज से हजारों साल पहले..."
2. **The golden age:** what life was like, meaning the culture, trade, learning, travel and
   faith, with specific names (ह्वेनसांग, नालंदा, Silk Route ...). Make it feel lived-in.
3. **The common people's view (encouraged):** a short passage on how an ordinary farmer,
   trader, monk or soldier lived and felt. Keep it rooted in history ("उस समय एक आम किसान
   के लिए..."). Don't invent named characters unless they are clearly framed as an example
   ("मान लीजिए उस समय का एक व्यापारी...").
4. **Outsiders arrive:** who came first, how they came in (trade, war, treaties), and how they
   slowly weakened, looted or ruled. The invaders' or rulers' motives can be shown too ("वहीं
   अगर अंग्रेजों की नजर से देखें तो...").
5. **Recovery / independence:** where each side stood at the moment the modern story begins.
   Use "इधर ... वहीं ..." to run parallel timelines.
6. **Myth-buster line**, if it fits: what the audience wrongly assumes. ("यानी आज के इस विवाद
   को सदियों पुरानी नफ़रत कहना बिल्कुल सही नहीं होगा।")
7. **End on calm-before-storm:** the last lines show things looking good or stable, so that the
   foreshadowing line opening the Mid hits harder.

Rules:
- **Don't name or explain the main conflict yet.** Mention the actors, but not the problem.
- **Every sentence must come out of the one before it.** No "अब बात करते हैं..." jumps here.
  Moving forward in time is the transition.
- Facts must be real. Don't invent dates, rulers or numbers. If a detail is uncertain, leave
  it out or hedge it ("कहा जाता है").

## 3. MID (the spark, the escalation, and today)

**Job:** because of the Beginning, the viewer now knows how we got here. Name the topic,
find the first spark, and connect every dot up to the present.

Build it like this:
1. **Foreshadowing pivot (first line):** turn the calm into trouble. "लेकिन ये ... बहुत ही जल्द
   ... में बदलने वाला था, और इसके <N> कारण थे, जिनमें सबसे बड़ा कारण बना ..."
2. **Global context that lit the spark:** the larger forces that made it happen, such as the
   Cold War (USA vs USSR), a third country (Pakistan, Britain ...), ideology, resources,
   geography. Explain why each mattered to *this* story in one or two lines.
3. **Cause 1 → Cause 2 → Cause 3, each one a mini-story:**
   - Set up the background ("X को जड़ से समझने के लिए Y की हिस्ट्री को जानना बहुत जरूरी है।")
   - Side A's view, then "वहीं अगर <B> की साइड से समझे तो..."
   - What happened (year in words, numbers, names)
   - A consequence line linking it to today ("और यही ... आज ... की सबसे बड़ी वजह में से एक है।")
   - A bridge question into the next cause ("लेकिन इसके बावजूद ... आखिर ... कैसे?") or
     "अब आते हैं ... दूसरा सबसे बड़ा कारण।"
   - Keep the most sensitive or ongoing cause for last ("अब आते हैं तीसरी और बेहद ही sensitive
     कारण...").
4. **The flashpoint:** the war, crash, deal or event, told in story form with real figures
   (casualties, money, dates).
5. **After the flashpoint, up to today:** agreements, repeated clashes, and recent activity
   from the last 1–3 years ("recently दो हजार ... में ..."). Use the latest facts available
   from research.
6. Make the deadlock visible with a parallel or mirror line where you can.

Rules:
- **Every claim must be backed by research** (official data, credible reporting, academic
  work). Don't name the source in the script.
- When numbers are disputed, give both sides' figures, or hedge with "कुछ रिपोर्ट्स के मुताबिक".
- Explain each technical term in one plain line the first time ("उसी को L A C कह दिया").
- The Mid should feel like *one* chain of cause and effect, not a list of facts.

## 4. CONCLUSION (short, forward-looking, an open question)

**Job:** say what it means now and what might happen next, then leave the viewer thinking.
**Don't repeat or summarise** anything.

Pick one or two of these:
- **Impact:** what this means for India's security, economy, or ordinary people ("...जो भारत
  की सुरक्षा के लिए बड़ा खतरा है").
- **Fact-based prediction:** where current trends point, with the other side's claim included
  ("हालांकि <X> का कहना है कि...", "अब देखना है भारत कैसे इस आने वाली खतरे से निपटता है।").
- **Closing question, always included:** start with "लेकिन आप सब से एक सवाल है, ..." and pose
  a real paradox or open question that came out of the research, then **restate it once in
  plainer words** with "या कहे तो ...". End right there. No outro and no CTA.

Example shape:
> लेकिन आप सब से एक सवाल है, <paradox>? या कहे तो <same question, simpler> |
