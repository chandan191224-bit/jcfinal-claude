# The creator's voice: style guide

These notes come from the creator's own script (`sample-script-india-china.md`) and from how
they describe their process. Read the whole file before writing. The sample script is the
final word on voice.

---

## 1. Who is speaking, and to whom

- **Narrator persona:** a storyteller friend who has read a lot and explains it plainly. Not
  a news anchor and not a professor. Confident, a little dramatic, never preachy.
- **Audience:** ordinary Hindi-speaking YouTube viewers who are curious but not experts.
  Nothing is assumed. Every term, treaty or place gets explained in one easy line the first
  time it appears.
- **Address:** the viewer is spoken to directly, though not often. "दोस्तों" is used once or
  twice in the whole script, never in every paragraph. "आप सब" appears in the closing question.
  Collective "हम" is used for things like "हमे इतिहास के पन्नों में झांकना होगा".
- **Show name:** "the favorite Show". It is mentioned only once, at the end of the hook.

## 2. Language: Hinglish written in Devanagari

This is the biggest marker of the voice. Get it right.

- **Base language:** everyday spoken Hindi with Hindustani/Urdu vocabulary. Avoid heavy
  Sanskritised "शुद्ध हिंदी".
  - Use: कब्जा, हुकूमत, हवाला, मौजूदगी, नतीजा, तरफदारी, दादागीरी, झड़प, शहीद, शरण, मेहमान,
    सख्त चेतावनी, ताकतवर, मशहूर, तकरीबन, आखिरकार, बिना देरी किए
  - Avoid: अधिग्रहण, संप्रभुता, आक्रांता (prefer "invaders"), द्विपक्षीय, परिप्रेक्ष्य, तत्पश्चात्
- **English words, two ways, both on purpose:**
  1. **In Latin script**, for words that should feel punchy or "modern":
     `time waste`, `confusing story`, `civilization`, `rich culture`, `cold war`, `agreement`,
     `Forward Policy`, `geographical advantage`, `invaders`, `spiritual guru`, `sensitive`,
     `protect`, `disturb`, `recently`, `use`, `dam`, `business`, `region`, `western countries`
  2. **In Devanagari**, for English words that have become everyday Hindi:
     बॉर्डर, बिजनेस, इकॉनोमी, डेवलपमेंट, हिस्ट्री, लिस्ट, पोस्ट, पिलर, बफर स्टेट, मेन फोकस,
     इंटरनल मैटर, टेंशन, रिकॉर्ड, कन्ट्रोल, चाइनीज ट्रैवलर्स, कम्युनिस्ट पार्टी
  - Mixing within one phrase is normal: "नेचुरल blessings", "chicken नेक", "World war टू".
  - The same word can show up both ways in one script ("business" / "बिजनेस", "border" /
    "बॉर्डर"). Don't force them to match.
  - Aim for roughly **1 English word in every 8–12 words**. Don't overdo it. The sentence
    should still sound like Hindi with English sprinkled in, not English with Hindi glue.
- **Country names as people say them:** "चाइना" more often than "चीन" (both appear), "रसिया"
  (not "रूस"), "अमेरिका", "Japan"/"जापान", "Asia"/"एशिया".
- **Grammar should sound spoken, not polished.** The creator writes the way people talk:
  "इसका तीन कारण था", "भारत पे हमला", "तिब्बत पे कब्जा", "जिसके वजह से". Use "पे" as freely
  as "पर". Don't "correct" spoken constructions into textbook Hindi. Still, avoid outright
  typos; a voiceover artist will read this.

## 3. Numbers and acronyms: written for the voiceover

The script is read aloud, so it is written the way it should sound.

- **Years are always written out in words:** "उन्नीस सौ सैंतालीस", "उन्नीस सौ बासठ",
  "दो हजार सत्रह", "दो हजार बीस", "सत्रह सौ बीस", "तेरहवीं सदी", "सातवीं सदी".
  Never write a year as "1947".
- **Other figures use digits plus spoken units:** "२ तिहाई", "२ सौ साल", "१ महीने",
  "१३ सौ ८३ सैनिक", "१७ सौ से ज्यादा", "20 से 30 किलोमीटर". Big numbers are split the way
  they are spoken: "१३ सौ ८३", not "१३८३". Devanagari (१२३) or Latin (123) digits are both fine.
- **Acronyms are spaced so they are read letter by letter:** "L A C", "एल ए सी". Give the full
  form once in brackets the first time it appears.
- **Approximations are welcome:** लगभग, करीब, तकरीबन, से ज्यादा, सैकड़ों, हजारों.

## 4. Sentence rhythm

- Medium-length sentences joined with **commas**, the way people speak. One idea flows into
  the next: "..., ताकि ...", "..., और साथ ही ...", "..., जो ...".
- Mix in **very short punch lines** on their own line for impact:
  - "इसलिए तिब्बत भारत के लिए एक सुरक्षा कवच जैसा था।"
  - "चलिए समझते हैं।"
  - "अब आते है दुश्मनी का दूसरा सबसे बड़ा कारण।"
- Use the danda "।" for full stops and "?" for rhetorical questions. A heading-like line can
  stand alone, for example `एल ए सी (Line of Actual Control)`.
- Paragraphs are short, 1–5 sentences each, and each one is one beat of the story.

## 5. Signature devices (use them, and spread them out)

| Device | Example from the creator | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Foreshadowing line** | "लेकिन ये भाई भाई का नारा बहुत ही जल्द दुश्मनी में बदलने वाला था" | End of Beginning → opens Mid |
| **Counted causes** | "और इसका तीन कारण था ... सबसे बड़ा कारण बना..." then "अब आते है दूसरा सबसे बड़ा कारण" | To structure the Mid |
| **Rhetorical bridge question** | "लेकिन इस agreement के बावजूद दोस्ती आखिर टूटी कैसे?" | Between two causes or phases |
| **Myth-buster** | "यानी आज के इस विवाद को सदियों पुरानी नफ़रत कहना बिल्कुल सही नहीं होगा।" | After setting up the history |
| **Two-side POV** | "वहीं अगर चाइना की साइड से समझे तो, ..." | Whenever there is a dispute; always give the other side |
| **Parallel / mirror lines** | "भारत जहां गश्त करता है, चीन कहता है तुम हमारी तरफ आ गए। चीन जहां टेंट लगाता है, भारत कहता है तुम हमारी तरफ आ गए।" | To make a deadlock easy to see |
| **Dialogue paraphrase** | "दोनों देशों ने कहा, चलो जब तक बॉर्डर तय नहीं होता, हम जहाँ हैं वहीं शांति रखेंगे।" | Putting treaties and policies into plain words |
| **Quoted slogan** | चीन ने "सौ साल में ... जो इलाके खोए हैं, वो सब वापस लाने हैं।" | When a leader's or regime's slogan explains its motive |
| **Vivid metaphor** | सुरक्षा कवच, दुश्मनी की आग में झोंक दिया, खून का दाग, आग धधकती रहती है, हिमालय की दीवार, दादागीरी | 1–2 per section, no more |
| **"Here vs there" timeline** | "इधर उन्नीस सौ सैंतालीस में नेहरू ..." / "वहीं चीन ..." | Showing what each side did at the same time |
| **Consequence line** | "और यही सड़क आज भारत-चीन झगड़े की सबसे बड़ी वजह में से एक है।" | Linking an old event to today |
| **"चलिए समझते हैं"** | after posing a puzzle | Before explaining something tricky |
| **Paradox opener** | "एक बॉर्डर जिसे आज तक किसी ने नहीं देखा।" | Hook |

## 6. Intensifiers and connectors (the glue words)

- **Intensifiers:** बेहद, बेहद ही, काफी, बहुत ही, बहुत बड़ा, बुरी तरह, हमेशा के लिए, बिल्कुल,
  सीधे, तेजी से
- **Connectors**, which carry the smooth flow: तो, लेकिन, वहीं, इधर, यानी, इसलिए, साथ ही,
  इसके अलावा, हालांकि, आखिरकार, नतीजा, और इसी का हवाला देते हुए, इसके बाद, उसी दिन से,
  और इस के चलते ही, बिना देरी किए
- **Topic-turn phrases:** "अब आते हैं ...", "चलिए समझते हैं", "इसे जड़ से समझने के लिए ...
  जानना बहुत जरूरी है", "इस पूरी कहानी को समझने के लिए हमे इतिहास के पन्नों में झांकना
  होगा दोस्तों"

## 7. Tone

- **Dramatic but grounded.** The emotion comes from the facts (deaths, betrayal, lost
  friendship). Exclamation marks and shouting are not needed.
- **Fair.** Every dispute gets both sides' claims ("भारत ... मानता है, जबकि चीन नहीं मानता").
  The narrator may say where India's interest lies, but never insults another country or
  community.
- **Slightly emotional about India** (शहीद, सुरक्षा कवच टूटना), but analytical in the conclusion.
- **Curious.** It ends with an open question, not a sermon.

## 8. Things the creator never does

- Never asks viewers to like, subscribe, comment, share or "watch till the end". The urge to
  keep watching has to come from the story alone.
- Never names sources ("according to Reuters", "Wikipedia says"). Facts are stated plainly,
  or softly hedged with "कहा जाता है" or "कुछ रिपोर्ट्स के मुताबिक" when they are disputed.
- Never jumps abruptly. A new paragraph always grows out of the last line of the one before.
- Never uses bullet points, headings or markdown inside the spoken script (only the
  standalone term line, like `एल ए सी (...)`).
- Never repeats a summary in the conclusion.
- Never uses stage directions or visual cues inside the narration (B-roll notes go in a
  separate optional block, and only if the user asks).
- Never uses emojis.
