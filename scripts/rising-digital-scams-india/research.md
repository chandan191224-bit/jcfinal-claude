# Research: Rising digital scams in India, from online to phone call

Defaults used: 6–7 min (~1,450 words), India's POV, focus on common people.

## Fact sheet

### Deep history (Beginning)
- English "thug" / "thuggee" comes from Hindi ठग / ठगी (thief, swindler; Sanskrit *sthagati*, "to conceal"). It entered English around 1830–40. https://www.dictionary.com/browse/Thuggee , https://www.npr.org/sections/codeswitch/2013/11/18/245953619/what-a-thugs-life-looked-like-in-nineteenth-century-india
- William Sleeman led the anti-Thuggee campaign in the 1830s. Nearly 4,000 suspected Thugs were uncovered and about 2,000 convicted. Sleeman's 1836 *Ramaseeana* recorded their secret language. (NPR, above)
- Caveat: historians (van Woerkens, PMLA) argue the British partly exaggerated or "invented" the cult. Hedge this in the script ("कई इतिहासकार मानते हैं..."). https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/pmla/article/abs/thug-the-barbarian-and-the-work-of-injury-in-imperial-warfare/9AD3FAC05196C2E7A5BD18D66A84F885
- Natwarlal (Mithilesh Kumar Srivastava) is said to have "sold" the Taj Mahal (3 times by some accounts), the Red Fort, Rashtrapati Bhavan and Parliament by posing as an official and forging signatures. He faced 100+ cases across 8 states and used 50+ aliases. These stories are partly legend, so hedge with "कहा जाता है". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natwarlal , https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-voices/catch-me-if-you-can-the-man-who-sold-the-taj-mahal

### Digital boom (end of Beginning)
- Jio launched in September 2016. Average data price fell from ₹226/GB (2015) to ₹75.57 (2016), ₹19.35 (2017) and ₹11.70 (2018). https://www.boomlive.in/fact-check/politics/mobile-data-prices-jio-modi-reliance-ambani-4g-tariff-per-gb-20141
- UPI processed 21.63 billion transactions worth ₹27.97 lakh crore in December 2025, its highest month ever. The 2025 total was 228+ billion transactions. https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/upi-ends-2025-on-a-high-with-record-monthly-and-annual-transactions-with-plans-for-future-initiatives-509218-2026-01-02

### Cause 1: Jamtara and the literacy gap
- Jamtara recorded 4 cases in 2013, 7 in 2014 and 12 in 2015. Police only understood how the scheme worked in June 2015, after arresting a 21-year-old. https://lawfullegal.in/jamtara-the-phishing-capital-of-india/?amp=1
- Method: callers posed as bank officials, threatened to block the victim's ATM card or account, and got card details and OTPs out of them. https://www.business-standard.com/article/finance/banks-told-to-tighten-monitoring-have-ironclad-kyc-to-avoid-new-jamtara-122021601581_1.html
- The Netflix series "Jamtara" came out in 2020.
- FCRF study (2023): 80% of cybercrime comes from 10 districts. Bharatpur 18%, Mathura 12%, Nuh 11%, Deoghar 10%, Jamtara 9.6%. Common factors are poverty, few jobs, low digital literacy and proximity to cities. https://www.deccanherald.com/india/80-cybercrimes-from-10-districts-in-country-bharatpur-new-jamtara-study-2699016 , https://www.wionews.com/india-news/mathura-bharatpur-pass-jamtara-nuh-as-infamous-cybercrime-hubs-of-india-says-study-639421

### Cause 2: the international scam industry
- 6,998 Indians were rescued from cybercrime compounds in Cambodia (2,533), Laos (2,297) and Myanmar (2,168) between 2022 and 2025. The government says it does not know how many are still stranded. https://scroll.in/latest/1090539/nearly-7000-indians-rescued-from-cambodia-myanmar-laos-cybercrime-compounds-since-2022-centre
- After the October 2025 raid on KK Park in Myanmar, IAF C-130J planes brought back 270 Indians, and 467 had returned by 10 November. https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/myanmar-s-raid-on-kk-park-unpacking-the-implications-for-india
- People are lured with fake job offers and forced to scam others through social media apps. https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/cambodia-360-indians-rescued-from-scam-compounds-after-being-lured-and-forced-to-use-social-media-apps-to-scam-victims/
- MHA (via The420): about ₹1,000 crore a month is lost to frauds run from Southeast Asia. This is a secondary source, so hedge it. https://the420.in/india-monthly-cyber-fraud-losses-rs1000-crore-southeast-asia-scam-networks/
- In 2025, investment scams made up more than 75% of money lost. https://theprint.in/india/cybercrime-saw-24-spike-in-2025-indians-lost-rs-22495-crore-mainly-in-investment-scams/2859930/

### Cause 3: fear and trust (digital arrest, AI voice)
- Digital arrest cases in 2024: 1,23,672 (up 103%), with ₹1,918 crore lost (up 465%). In 2025: 17,264 cases (down 86%) and ₹644 crore lost (down 66.4%). https://theprint.in/india/governance/after-465-spike-in-2024-mha-data-shows-digital-arrest-scams-are-on-a-decline-in-india/2857002/
- An 86-year-old Mumbai woman lost over ₹20 crore across two months in early 2025. A Bengaluru engineer lost ₹11.8 crore. (Same search, Deccan Herald)
- Mann Ki Baat, 27 October 2024: no agency questions people by phone or video call, "digital arrest" does not exist in law, the advice is "रुको, सोचो, एक्शन लो", and fraud should be reported to 1930. https://organiser.org/2024/10/27/262496/bharat/mann-ki-baat-pm-modi-warns-about-digital-arrest/
- McAfee 2023: 47% of Indians had faced an AI voice scam or knew someone who had (global average 25%). 83% of Indian victims lost money, and 69% can't tell an AI voice from a real one. https://www.boomlive.in/explainers/ai-voice-scam-voice-cloning-mcafee-report-21851

### Flashpoint and today
- Losses: ₹7,465 crore in 2023 and ₹22,845 crore in 2024, roughly 3 times as much (MHA in Lok Sabha). https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/cyber-fraud-costs-indians-22-845-crore-in-2024-government
- 2025: ₹22,495 crore lost and 28.15 lakh cases, up from 22.68 lakh in 2024. https://theprint.in/india/cybercrime-saw-24-spike-in-2025-indians-lost-rs-22495-crore-mainly-in-investment-scams/2859930/
- Government side: 73 lakh mobile connections taken on fake documents were disconnected (Lok Sabha, August 2024). Chakshu on Sanchar Saathi is for reporting fraud calls and SMS. https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2979756/1/AU2523_80Hi7g.pdf
- I4C projection (secondary source): more than ₹1.2 lakh crore could be lost in 2025. This is a projection, not a measured figure, so hedge it or leave it out. https://the420.in/india-cyber-fraud-52976-crore-i4c-data-investment-scams/

## Story chain (outline)

**Beginning**
1. Entry: the word "thug" comes from our own ठग. → bridge: who these ठग were
2. Thuggee in the 1830s. Travellers on roads, Sleeman, about 2,000 convicted, with the historians' caveat. → bridge: the ठग needed your face and your road
3. Common people's view: an ordinary traveller feared the road, and the con was face to face. → bridge: in the 20th century the ठग traded the road for paper
4. Natwarlal: forged signatures and the "sold" Taj Mahal. He still needed the victim in front of him. → bridge: then the phone arrived
5. Lottery SMS and email. → bridge: but the real change came in 2016
6. Calm before the storm: Jio data, UPI, QR codes, a whole bank in every pocket.

**Mid**
1. Foreshadow: the same phone becomes a weapon. Three causes, starting with Jamtara.
2. Global context: the whole world moving online, COVID (kept general).
3. Cause 1, Jamtara: fake bank calls and OTPs, then the scammers' side (poverty, no jobs), then it spreads to Bharatpur, Nuh and Mathura. → bridge question: how did a village game become an international business?
4. Cause 2, Southeast Asia compounds: fake jobs, captive Indians made to scam Indians, a mirror line, investment scams, about 7,000 rescued, the KK Park raid. → "अब आते हैं तीसरी और बेहद sensitive कारण"
5. Cause 3, digital arrest and AI voice cloning: fear and trust, with real cases.
6. Flashpoint 2024: losses triple and digital arrest cases pass 1 lakh, then Mann Ki Baat, then the government's measures.
7. 2025: digital arrest falls 86% but total losses stay the same, as investment scams take over. Mirror line: one door shuts, another window opens.

**Conclusion**
- Impact and prediction: the more UPI grows, the more people are at risk. AI makes voices and faces fakeable. The government's projection is hedged.
- Question: the easier technology makes sending money, the easier it becomes to lose it. Why?
