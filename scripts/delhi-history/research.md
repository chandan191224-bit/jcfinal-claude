## Fact sheet

### Ancient Delhi
- Purana Qila is traditionally identified with Indraprastha, the Pandava capital in the Mahabharata.
  The link is a tradition, not proven by archaeology.
  ([Drishti IAS, 2 Jun 2023](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/purana-qila-excavation))
- ASI digs at Purana Qila (2013-14, 2017-18, 2023) found 9 cultural levels: Pre-Mauryan, Mauryan,
  Sunga, Kushana, Gupta, Post-Gupta, Rajput, Sultanate, Mughal. Also a Mauryan-era well about
  2,500 years old and Painted Grey Ware shards (dated c. 1200–600 BCE).
  ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/purana-qila-excavation),
  [Governance Now](https://www.governancenow.com/news/regular-story/new-excavation-season-to-begin-at-delhis-purana-qila))
- An Ashokan rock edict at Srinivaspuri / East of Kailash was found in 1966, when a contractor noticed
  writing on a rock that was about to be blasted.
  ([Wikipedia: Ashokan Edicts in Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashokan_Edicts_in_Delhi))
- The Iron Pillar in Mehrauli is about 1,600 years old, weighs more than 6 tonnes and stands ~7.2 m,
  with ~1 m below ground. Its inscription names a king "Chandra", usually identified as Chandragupta II.
  It still hasn't rusted.
  ([KRDO/CNN 2024](https://krdo.com/news/2024/05/06/this-mysterious-iron-pillar-in-india-has-been-exposed-to-the-elements-for-over-1600-years-so-why-hasnt-it-ever-rusted/),
  [LiveHistoryIndia](https://livehistoryindia.com/story/monuments/iron-pillar))

### Tomars and Chauhans
- Anangpal Tomar (II) is credited with founding Dhillika/Dhilli and building Lal Kot, c. 1052–1060 CE.
  A line on the Iron Pillar reads "Samvat Dihali 1109", which Cunningham read as 1052 CE.
  Some traditions put the dynasty's start in 736 CE (Anangpal I). Much of this comes from bardic legend.
  ([Wikipedia: Anangpal Tomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anangpal_Tomar),
  [Tomaras of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomaras_of_Delhi),
  [Lal Kot / Qila Rai Pithora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qila_Rai_Pithora))
- The "Dhillika" name appears in Bijolia and other Sanskrit inscriptions.
  The "ढीली कीली" origin story is a folk legend, so it gets hedged.
- The Chauhans took Delhi in the 12th century. Prithviraj Chauhan expanded Lal Kot into Qila Rai Pithora.
  He beat Muhammad Ghori at Tarain in 1191 and lost to him there in 1192.
  Qutbuddin Aibak entered Lal Kot after that. ([Qila Rai Pithora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qila_Rai_Pithora))

### Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526)
- Aibak built the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque (c. 1193). His own inscription says material came from
  27 Hindu and Jain temples. He began the Qutub Minar, and Iltutmish added three storeys.
  Firoz Shah Tughlaq later repaired it after a lightning strike. Height 72.5 m, 379 steps.
  The start date is given as 1192, 1193 or 1199.
  ([Wikipedia: Qutb Minar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_Minar),
  [Qutb complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_Minar_complex),
  [Delhi Tourism](https://delhitourism.gov.in/tourist_place/qutab_minar.html))
- Razia Sultan (r. 1236–1240) was the first woman to rule from Delhi's throne.
- Alauddin Khilji held off Mongol invasions (1297–1303), built Siri as a garrison city, and fixed
  prices in Delhi's markets with officers (shahna) and grain stores.
  ([Anantam IAS notes](https://www.anantamias.com/delhi-sultanate/),
  [AEES handout](https://www.aees.gov.in/htmldocs/downloads/e-content_06_04_20/VII_HIS_L03_M03_DELHI_SULTANS_HANDOUT.pdf))
- Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq built Tughlaqabad. Two legends: Nizamuddin Auliya's "हनूज़ दिल्ली दूर अस्त",
  and the curse "या रहे उजड़, या बसे गूजर". Both are hedged as कहा जाता है.
- Muhammad bin Tughlaq moved the capital to Daulatabad (~1,100 km away) in 1327 and later moved it back.
  His token copper currency (1329–30) failed because of forgery.
  ([Anantam IAS](https://www.anantamias.com/delhi-sultanate/))
- Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351–88) built Firozabad (Kotla). He brought an Ashokan pillar from Topra
  (near Ambala) on a 42-wheel cart and by boat down the Yamuna.
  ([Wikipedia: Major Pillar Edicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_Pillar_Edicts))
- Timur sacked Delhi on 17 Dec 1398. He ordered about 100,000 captives killed. Britannica says
  "possibly around 100,000", so the figure gets hedged. Chroniclers wrote that for two months
  "not a bird moved its wing" in the city.
  ([Wikipedia: Sack of Delhi 1398](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Delhi_(1398)),
  [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/2096027))
- Lodis: Sikandar Lodi moved the capital to Agra (c. 1506). The Lodi tombs still stand in Lodi Garden.

### Mughals
- Babur beat Ibrahim Lodi at Panipat in 1526.
- Humayun built Dinpanah (1533). Sher Shah Suri drove him out (1540) and built Shergarh on its ruins,
  now Purana Qila. ([Delhi Tourism: Shergarh](https://www.delhitourism.gov.in/aboutus/shergarh.html))
- Humayun came back in 1555 and died in January 1556 after falling on the stairs of the Sher Mandal
  (popular account; some historians dispute it). His widow built Humayun's Tomb. Sources disagree
  between Bega Begum and Hamida Banu, so the script says "बेगम".
  ([Wikipedia: Humayun's tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humayun%27s_tomb))
- Hemu beat the Mughals at Tughlaqabad on 7 Oct 1556, took Delhi and took the title Vikramaditya.
  He lost the Second Battle of Panipat on 5 Nov 1556 and was executed.
  ([Wikipedia: Second Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Panipat),
  [Battle of Tughlaqabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tughlaqabad))
- Shah Jahan: Red Fort foundation 29 Apr 1639, completed 1648 (Shahjahanabad). Jama Masjid 1650–56
  with about 5,000 workers. Chandni Chowk (1650) was designed by Jahanara Begum; moonlight on its
  central pool and canal gave it the name.
  ([Wikipedia: Shahjahanabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahjahanabad),
  [Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/chandni-chowk-jahanaras-glorious-moonlight-square-turned-dust))
- Aurangzeb died in 1707, and the empire declined after that.
- Nadir Shah, March 1739: on 22 March, at Sunehri Masjid, he ordered a massacre after his soldiers were
  killed in a riot. Estimates are 20,000–30,000 killed in one day (uncertain). He took the Peacock
  Throne and the Koh-i-Noor.
  ([Wikipedia: Sunehri Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunehri_Masjid_(Chandni_Chowk)),
  [Nader Shah's invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah%27s_invasion_of_the_Mughal_Empire))
- Ahmad Shah Abdali raided Delhi several times (1757 and later). In 1771 Mahadji Shinde brought
  Shah Alam II back to Delhi. Folk saying: "सल्तनत-ए-शाह आलम, अज़ दिल्ली ता पालम".
- 1803, Battle of Delhi (Patparganj): the British beat the Marathas, and the Mughal emperor became
  a British pensioner.

### British Delhi
- 1857: Meerut sepoys reached Delhi on 11 May and made the 82-year-old Bahadur Shah Zafar their leader
  (his formal audience with them was on 12 May). British siege guns opened on 8 Sept and breached the
  walls by 14 Sept. Delhi was back under British control by 21 Sept after street fighting. Zafar's sons
  were shot by Hodson near Khooni Darwaza (well documented in the Dalrymple account). He was exiled
  to Rangoon in 1858 and died there on 7 Nov 1862, aged 87.
  ([Britannica: Siege of Delhi](https://www.britannica.com/event/Siege-of-Delhi),
  [Wikipedia: Bahadur Shah Zafar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahadur_Shah_Zafar))
- Couplet "कितना है बदनसीब ज़फ़र…" is commonly linked to Zafar, but the attribution is disputed.
- 12 Dec 1911, Delhi Durbar: George V announced the capital would move from Calcutta to Delhi.
  Reasons: unrest after the Partition of Bengal, central location, historic prestige.
  Lutyens and Baker designed it. Viceroy's House (Rashtrapati Bhavan) has 340 rooms.
  India Gate was dedicated on 12 Feb 1931. New Delhi was inaugurated by Lord Irwin on 13 Feb 1931.
  ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/india-s-capital-new-delhi-was-inaugurated-91-years-ago-news-122515),
  [LiveHistoryIndia](https://livehistoryindia.com/story/eras/new-delhi),
  [Heritage Portal](https://www.theheritageportal.co.za/article/edwin-lutyens-herbert-baker-and-imperial-delhi))
- 23 Dec 1912: bomb thrown at Viceroy Hardinge in Chandni Chowk. 8 Apr 1929: Bhagat Singh and
  Batukeshwar Dutt threw bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly.

### Independent Delhi
- 1947 partition: Delhi had ~9.18 lakh people in 1941 and ~17.4 lakh in 1951. About 4.7–5 lakh refugees
  arrived and about 3.2–3.3 lakh Muslims left. Refugees made up 28.4% of the 1951 population.
  Purana Qila and Humayun's Tomb became camps. New colonies: Lajpat Nagar, Rajinder Nagar,
  Punjabi Bagh, Kingsway Camp.
  ([The Wire](https://thewire.in/history/how-partition-reshaped-delhis-demography-language-and-culture),
  [1947 Partition Archive](https://in.1947partitionarchive.org/node/1809),
  [IRD study](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/divers14-11/010023836.pdf))
- Gandhi was assassinated at Birla House on 30 Jan 1948.
- Delhi became a Union Territory in 1956. The 69th Amendment (1991) made it the National Capital
  Territory, and its first assembly came in 1993.
- 1982 Asian Games: flyovers and stadiums were built, and colour TV broadcasting began.
- 1984 anti-Sikh riots: 2,733 killed in Delhi out of 3,325 nationwide (official figures).
  ([The Wire](https://thewire.in/politics/special-investigation-team-to-reopen-75-anti-sikh-riot-cases))
- Delhi Metro: first train 24 Dec 2002. In 2026 it runs ~374 km (core) or ~395–416 km counting
  Aqua and Rapid. About 217 km more is planned.
  ([Business Today, Jul 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/photo/delhi-metros-biggest-expansion-ever-217-km-123-new-stations-16-corridors-planned-under-phases-4-5a-and-5b-545145-2026-07-24))
- 2023: new Parliament building, and the G20 summit at Bharat Mandapam.
- Feb 2025 election: BJP 48, AAP 22, Congress 0 of 70. BJP returned after 27 years.
  Rekha Gupta was sworn in as CM on 20 Feb 2025.
  ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/2/20/rekha-gupta-of-indias-ruling-bjp-sworn-in-as-chief-minister-of-delhi))
- UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025 ranks New Delhi 4th in the world at ~30.2 million (3 crore),
  and projects ~33.9 million by 2050. The older 2018 projection, "largest by 2028", is outdated.
  ([UN DESA data booklet](https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/sites/www.un.org.development.desa.pd/files/undesa_pd_2025_data-booklet_world_cities_in_2025.pdf))
- Oct 2026: GRAP Stage 1 started on 21 Oct, with AQI 164–187 ("moderate"). Toxic foam is back on the
  Yamuna at Kalindi Kunj days after ~₹9,000 crore of Yamuna projects were inaugurated.
  ([Business Today, 25 Oct 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/toxic-foam-is-back-on-the-yamuna-and-delhi-ncr-aqi-has-reached-168-here-is-what-is-happening-563488-2026-10-25))

## Story chain

**Beginning** (calm, the city's roots)
1. Entry, then Yamuna and the Aravalli ridge: why this land drew people → bridge: "और यहीं से शुरू होती है इंद्रप्रस्थ की कहानी"
2. Indraprastha legend + Purana Qila digs (9 layers) → bridge: the soil remembers Mauryas
3. Ashoka edict 1966, then Gupta Iron Pillar → bridge: the pillar carries a line about Dhilli's founder
4. Tomars: Anangpal, Lal Kot, the Dhillika name, the ढीली कीली legend → bridge: the Chauhans take over
5. Common people's view: a trader or farmer in Qila Rai Pithora → calm before the storm: Prithviraj wins Tarain 1191

**Mid** (seven times ruined, seven times rebuilt; three big waves: Sultanate, Mughal, British)
- Pivot: "लेकिन ये जीत बहुत ही जल्द…", Tarain 1192, Aibak, Quwwat-ul-Islam, Qutub Minar
- Wave 1, Sultanate: Iltutmish and Razia → Khilji (Mongols, Siri, prices) → Tughlaq (curse, Daulatabad, token coins) → Firoz (pillar) → Timur 1398 (the low point) → Lodis leave for Agra
- Wave 2, Mughals: Babur 1526 → Humayun, Sher Shah, Purana Qila → Humayun's fall → Hemu 1556 → Shahjahanabad, Chandni Chowk, Jama Masjid (golden age) → Aurangzeb 1707 → Nadir Shah 1739 → Abdali, Mir's couplet → Shah Alam "दिल्ली ता पालम" → 1803 British
- Wave 3, the most sensitive one, the British: 1857 → Zafar exile → 1911 Durbar → New Delhi 1931 → revolutionaries 1912/1929 → 1947
- Flashpoint: partition, refugees, camps in the same monuments → Gandhi 1948
- Up to today: 1956/1991/1993 → 1982 → 1984 → Metro 2002 → 2023 Parliament and G20 → 2025 election → Oct 2026 AQI and foam

**Conclusion**: 3 crore people, 4th largest city (UN 2025), air and water.
Closing question: a city no invader could finish off, can it survive its own smoke and crowds?
